# Reader-first financial briefs

These rules govern every brief, email, chart, table, caption and accessible label.

## One company is a publication invariant

Every servicing brief and email draft covers exactly ONE reporting company and one earnings event. Identify that company explicitly in the report metadata, rendered document and email package, and verify that the identities agree. Never silently select the first company from mixed inputs or concatenate complete company briefs into the normal email body. Multiple companies reporting on the same day produce separate briefs and separate drafts. A directory may link to those individual documents; it is not a financial brief. Historical multi-company artifacts are not the default or a reusable exception.

This boundary does not exclude useful comparisons with the same company's compatible prior periods, relevant annual filings, or incidental references to another company in a sourced statement. Source meaning and the reporting entity determine the boundary, not a naive scan for other company names. A polished multi-company page still fails this publication rule.

The normal report generator and publication path use `servicing_brief/company_boundary.py` to require explicit company/event metadata, matching HTML identity and readable plain-text company/period headers. Validate every company output and any issuer-logo marker against that identity. Recheck the actual email alternatives before SMTP. Missing identity is a hold, not permission to skip the check. Keep CIKs and validation details out of the visible brief; the reader sees the company name, ticker and reporting period. Supporting annual and prior-period documents remain permitted when their issuer matches and their context role is explicit.

## Executive depth and reading order

Build a company review, not an isolated metric followed by a generic question. Start with a defensible synthesis of the quarter's central servicing, renewal, retention, credit or financing development. Put management's explanation and the reader's bounded business implication near the finding, with the exact supporting figures and sources easy to reach. Distinguish reported results, management expectations and analyst interpretation. Recover useful call discussion across prepared remarks and Q&A before deciding there is little to say.

Use enough supported detail to explain the important financial tradeoffs; do not manufacture length or suppress useful analysis merely to make the page short. A chart must earn its physical space. Company identity and its logo belong together in a compact masthead; the publisher is secondary. Use restrained brand accents, a clear tonal hierarchy and deliberate spacing so white space supports comprehension rather than separating related ideas across long blank stretches. Complete desktop, narrow-phone and email views must all pass independent Astra review.

## Questions and original AI Analysis

Use the heading **Questions**. Follow it with **AI Analysis**, then Sources, when original analysis has been authored for the current company and event. Give the public voice room to explain earnings, available call remarks, financial mechanics and mortgage-servicing implications from the source material. Use developed paragraphs and useful subheadings, with the shared readable text and light/dark palette. Do not impose an excerpt-only constraint or a fixed paragraph quota. Attribute management views and distinguish the AI's interpretation from reported results. Preserve supporting source hashes and claim rationale internally, keep citations beside the paragraphs they support, and apply fresh factual and actual-output reader-value review to the complete argument.

An essay must not carry forward into a different issuer, event or changed source package. Missing original analysis must not be filled with source excerpts under an AI Analysis label. Keep this section distinct from optional source-context selection.

## Company identity

**Make the company recognizable on every brief.** Use the reporting company's official logo and verified primary and supporting colors consistently on each standalone company page and across its HTML, mobile, email and generated PDF versions. Company identity is a core design requirement, not an optional decoration added after the analysis. Retain the shared reading structure so readers recognize the issuer and The Servicing Brief as the separate publisher. Historical compiled emails are archives, not a template exception to the one-company rule.

Place the company name in real text beside its proportionate logo in a compact masthead. Follow with the ticker, reporting period and event date. Apply the verified palette across the full page: exact primary color in the summary panel, supporting colors in accents, and restrained derived tints for page and data surfaces. Long passages and exact figures need readable contrast. Do not copy an issuer's website navigation or add decorative components merely to imitate its site. On a generated PDF that spans physical pages, use a compact company running header so continuation pages remain identifiable without repeating the full opening. Preserve issuer-provided source documents unchanged.

Use issuer branding for the overall page identity, including its large filled summary panel. Preserve legible contrast, the logo's proportions and required clear space. A red or green corporate identity must not be mistaken for a financial risk or improvement signal. Keep chart and metric semantics separate from branding. Source logos and colors from the issuer, preserve provenance and original assets, and never invent a logo or claim an unverified palette is official.

Email must remain understandable with images blocked: keep the full company name as real text and avoid essential information inside images. A logo directly beside that name may use empty alternative text to avoid repeating the name or clipping text inside a narrow blocked-image box; the verified descriptive asset metadata stays in the registry. If the image is the only identity, meaningful alternative text is required. If verified assets are unavailable, use the company name and neutral styling while recording the branding gap internally. Branding is part of every loop's visual acceptance, including the normal single-company brief, mobile layout and email fallback.

Use the packaged email HTML for browser-based email composition. Packaging converts styled `section` containers to `div` containers because Gmail can retain a section's colors in compose but remove its wrapper and styling in the received message. Standalone reports keep their semantic sections. When an authorized sent message is available, inspect its received rendering as well as the compose preview and record any difference; do not treat compose approval as evidence of identical received styling.

When adding an issuer, confirm the reporting entity, obtain artwork from its official website or a linked asset host, verify the palette, and record the source URLs, valid ISO review date and original/PNG hashes in the shared branding registry. Keep the original artwork and use an unchanged, proportionate PNG rendering for email. Validate the actual PNG, not just its filename or signature. An invalid or unsupported asset follows the same neutral fallback as missing branding. Recheck the issuer's identity and artwork when its reporting name, ownership or brand changes. Never recolor the logo to fit our page.

Branding acceptance covers the actual output for that issuer: correct company/logo pairing, verified colors, balanced placement, readable contrast, intact proportions, narrow-phone layout, blocked-image fallback and embedded email artwork. Check generated PDF continuation pages when present. Use the shared registry rather than separate hard-coded palettes for each format. Keep asset-verification notes and missing-brand reasons internal. Astra's review must assess these checks alongside the financial meaning and reader-value rules; a recognizable logo alone cannot make an otherwise weak brief acceptable.

## P0 publication requirement: prove reader value

This requirement takes priority over template completeness and earlier visual approval. A correct number, a new quotation, a reviewed document, and an attractive chart are not sufficient reasons to publish an item.

Before publication, review every headline clause, paragraph fact, chart, row, question, qualification and source entry. Ask: **What useful understanding or decision would the reader lose if this item disappeared?** State the concrete servicing earnings, cash flow, valuation, borrower risk, renewal/retention, or operating execution implication. Orientation and qualifications earn space when they prevent a specific misunderstanding. Generic statements such as "adds context" are not an editorial justification.

Compare the item with the whole brief. Repeated chart/table values can serve visual comprehension and exact lookup; repeated prose needs its own purpose. A direction of change alone is not materiality. Do not infer servicing quality from loan balances, absolute arrears, or broader bank profits. Preserve the actual dates, geography, population, weighting and denominator. Explain outlook as expectations, not achieved results.

Broader bank results, universe eligibility, static averages and annual mixed-business disclosures need an evidenced connection to the retained finding. They cannot be the primary servicing result. A scope disclaimer does not create that connection. Questions must test a material uncertainty in a retained finding. No useful finding means hold the analytical brief for review, not fill its slots. A concise coverage note may be the appropriate editorial result; it needs explicit review before publication.

Mortgage arrears and delinquency can be useful primary borrower-risk findings when the actual mortgage population, denominator, dates and monitoring question are clear. Judge the specific credit signal on its reader value. Do not require an unrelated fee-income figure merely to fill an analytical slot, and do not imply that a credit balance or rate proves servicing quality, collections cost or profitability. Neutral section labels must describe what was observed; a payment decrease alone does not justify calling it payment pressure.

Preserve complete canonical evidence internally. Publish only the useful selection. Do not require a fixed number of rows, insights, charts, questions, or positive/negative highlights. Document omissions in editorial configuration; they must never resurrect through canonical fallback. Remove unused source-list entries while keeping sources needed for retained claims and limitations. Reasons for removal stay internal. The reader sees findings and necessary qualifications, not the research checklist.

### Enforcement and review records

`servicing_brief/reader_value.py` inventories actual HTML and validates a named review bound to its exact SHA-256. Each item requires `keep`, `revise`, or `remove`, its reader need, incremental value, inclusion logic, scope, priority, and reason. The reviewer examines every clause within a paragraph/chart/row block. Any unresolved revision/removal, missing item or stale output blocks release. An analytical brief also requires a primary finding. The narrow `document_kind: coverage_note` exception must be visibly labeled, contain exactly one company, omit financial charts/tables and primary claims, and retain a sourced `coverage_boundary` paragraph. The same contract applies to its plain-text alternative. A reviewer must judge the argument; schema and word-count checks cannot establish meaning or truth.

For ordinary reports, place the completed HTML record beside the draft as `reader-value-review.json` and review the plain-text alternative separately in `reader-value-text-review.json`. Use `reader_value_release.text_review_html(text)` to inventory escaped plain-text lines with the same schema. HTML approval cannot authorize different text in an email alternative. The pipeline records internal `reader-value-status.json`, checks again on send, and holds unapproved drafts without attempting SMTP. A blocked financial MIME draft carries an additional delivery hold header. This header is a safeguard in the supported workflow, not a security boundary against manual editing or external mail tools.

For the five-company package, records live in `output/five-company-review/reader-value-reviews/`: one per `{ticker}-review.html`, `combined-email.html`, and `gmail-body.html`, with matching JSON stems. Existing `.txt` companions also require `{stem}-text.json` records using the escaped-line inventory. Run `.venv/Scripts/python.exe -m servicing_brief.reader_value_release output/five-company-review`. Both email packaging and phone-PDF creation require this gate. Review any changed rendering again; individual approvals cannot authorize new compilation content. Continue the existing financial, source-insight, punctuation and visual checks separately.

Supported explicit omissions in the five-company overlay: `excluded_metrics` with zero-based `metric_index` and reason; `omit_sections` for deck, insights, servicing_context and question; `chart.omit: true` with reason; `excluded_sources` with source_id and reason. An explicit empty call text already suppresses the call. Excluded sources still cited by retained content are rejected. Omitting every useful finding does not grant publication approval.

### Meaning follows the source's definitions

Inspect the source footnotes and glossary with each retained metric. Population, denominator and observation date are part of the fact. For example, a balance-based delinquency rate that includes multi-family mortgages cannot be presented as a count of single-family borrowers. Put the short definition near the visual and retain the precise supporting pages internally. Use labels such as prepayments, delinquency and retention only for the measure actually disclosed.

Make a directional headline's measure and comparison unambiguous on first reading. State the period in the headline when several displayed periods could suggest conflicting directions. An immediately adjacent, clearly labeled two-period visual can establish a single comparison without repeating dates in the headline. A quarter-over-quarter improvement must not imply improvement over every displayed period. Label portfolio balances as period-end stocks and specify whether a delinquency rate uses loan count or balances. Highlight only supported changes against a compatible prior period, state that basis next to the figures, and leave missing, flat or incompatible comparisons neutral. Management's explanation for a change does not rename the reported metric: interest expense attributed to larger MSR financing balances is still the disclosed servicing interest expense.

Changing display units may rescale an explicitly supported amount within the same currency; it may not relabel a currency, convert money to a percentage, or infer a missing scale. The five-company renderer rejects incompatible or unverified unit requests. Never hide an access failure under a definitive absence claim: when an unavailable call could reflect blocked issuer access, preserve that bounded availability qualification near Sources. Avoid HTTP codes, retry logs and unrelated failures in reader copy.

1. **Make the company easier to understand.** Lead with the important development, explain what changed and why it matters to an investor, and retain the context needed to interpret it. Avoid repeating the same fact in several sections. Remove content that only describes our research process.
2. **Keep research decisions internal.** Reasons such as “why it adds nothing material,” review status, and exclusion decisions belong in the internal evidence record. They do not belong in the reader-facing brief.
3. **Never mislead the viewer.** Preserve the actual financial meaning: currency, units, signs, reporting period, comparison basis, business scope and material limitations. Missing is not zero. Broader mortgage lending is not servicing profit. Annual context is not a current-quarter result. Management outlook is not an achieved result. Chart proportions must represent the numbers, with clearly labeled periods and an honest baseline. Do not manufacture a positive highlight when the evidence does not support one.
4. **P0: No em dashes, from design through final release.** Reject literal U+2014, decoded entities and generated/escaped em dashes in source labels, headings, body copy, tables, charts, captions, navigation, accessible names, tooltips, browser titles, email subjects, both email alternatives and generated brief PDFs. Run the shared check on final output after rendering and packaging, then inspect that exact version during design review. Any occurrence fails review and blocks release until corrected and rechecked. An older or changed artifact cannot inherit a clean result. Preserve source addresses and original issuer documents. Resolve punctuation without changing financial meaning or silently changing a direct quotation.

   **P0: Parentheses identify negative financial values.** Display negative amounts, percentages, basis points and changes as `($77m)`, `(2.5%)` and `(12 bps)`, in prose, tables, charts, accessible descriptions, email alternatives and generated brief PDFs. Keep the negative underlying data, calculations, units, denominators and chart direction unchanged. Positive values and zero are not parenthesized. Parentheses never mean missing data. Design review checks accounting presentation and financial meaning; final output validation rejects minus-prefixed negative display values. Do not rewrite source URLs or original issuer documents to satisfy a display rule.
5. **Require Astra Max acceptance.** Review the actual complete desktop, mobile and email experience. Useful content, readable hierarchy, truthful visuals and coherent top-to-bottom flow are mandatory. Passing mechanical checks alone is insufficient.
6. **Make the entire page belong to its company.** Each brief carries the correct official logo and verified palette across its full page, mobile view, email and generated PDF. The main summary panel uses that issuer's primary color, with contrast-tested foreground and citations. Page backgrounds, section surfaces, headings, links and rules use restrained tints or shades derived from the same palette. A shared green or navy panel must never remain from another company or a default theme. Keep the company recognizable through real text, preserve legibility, and separate corporate colors from financial improvement or risk signals. Follow the Company identity rules above, including the neutral fallback when assets cannot be verified.

The shared `branding.page_theme` function supplies all format colors. Exact official colors and derived surface tints are distinguished in its data. `validate_page_theme` checks required page, paper and summary surfaces against the current issuer registry, including inline summary text and citation colors. Report rendering, email preparation and the actual MIME send boundary enforce the check for themed briefs. Rendering tests must cover every registered issuer, absent/invalid branding, another issuer's substituted panel, dark and light foreground selection, and stripped-stylesheet email fallbacks. Branding approval requires inspecting the actual full page, not merely finding a correct logo or accent line.

### Light and dark readability

Use the shared semantic palette for all text, surfaces, financial highlights, chart bars, meaningful zero lines, logo plates and appended document links. `validate_theme_contrast` rejects either light or dark palettes below 7:1 for body text and financial highlight figures, 4.5:1 for notes and links, or 3:1 for informative chart elements. The exact issuer summary color uses whichever of black or white has greater contrast, with a 4.5:1 minimum. Decorative rules do not carry data. Keep original logo pixels on an opaque light plate.

`apply_page_theme` binds the rendered inline colors to literal dark CSS and supplies email `bgcolor` fallbacks. Keep foreground and background pairs explicit. Do not rely on CSS variables, opacity, a media query alone, or a client keeping the document head. Apply the shared transform after the document footer is appended, so packaged email receives the same checks. Future issuers, neutral fallback, invalid registry fallback and extreme valid primaries must pass the same palette tests.

Inspect the actual candidate at desktop, 390px and 320px in authored light and dark modes, plus stylesheet-stripped and blocked-image email variants. Check computed text contrast, chart contrast and horizontal overflow. An authored browser dark view is not proof of Gmail or Outlook's automatic recoloring. Inspect the actual connected email client before sending and the delivered message afterward; document the tested client and any remaining limits without claiming universal rendering. Gmail's [supported CSS queries](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/design/css) differ from [WebKit dark-mode email support](https://webkit.org/blog/8840/dark-mode-support-in-webkit/). Contrast thresholds follow [WCAG text contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-enhanced.html) and [non-text contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html).

## Source insight control

An available call or prepared-remarks document must produce either a supported incremental finding or an internal, specific reason why it adds nothing material. A finding needs source identity, archived file hash, passage/location, statement type and investor relevance. An unavailable transcript must not be invented. A short availability note belongs near Sources when its absence affects what the reader can rely on.

Review the whole available document for relevant passages before deciding it adds nothing: prepared remarks and Q&A can discuss retention, renewals, expenses, valuation, liquidity, borrower risk and outlook in different sections. One unhelpful margin paragraph cannot establish that the call lacks useful retention commentary. Version 2 source records retain the scope of that review and each candidate passage's location, excerpt, include/exclude decision and specific reader-value reason. Those decisions stay internal. A schema can require the record; it cannot prove that every useful passage was found. Astra must challenge the actual source selection.

Use `SOURCE_INSIGHT_CONTROL.md` for the evidence schema. The five-company packaging gate compares the record with the actual rendered call paragraphs, including legitimately empty paragraphs. Internal reasons are never supplied to the presentation template.

## Acceptance and limits

Keep each citation visually attached to its claim. A superscript alone on the next phone line interrupts reading; bind it to the final word without keeping an entire paragraph unbreakable. Plain-text briefs use compact source numbers inline and retain the original URLs in Sources. Extraction line numbers and retry details stay in the evidence record. Test every actual standalone brief as well as the compilation at narrow phone widths, with styles removed and images blocked. The basic layout must be fluid before responsive CSS is applied.

Run the shared reader-content gate on HTML and plain text. Check accessible text as well as visible text. Apply the source-insight gate to reviewed editorial call content. Recheck actual numeric cells against immutable evidence and inspect rendered chart geometry. Keep the email body below 90,000 bytes and verify its inline-only mobile fallback.

Automated checks detect known failures; they cannot prove that prose is useful or that an interpretation is faithful. Source review and Astra's visual/editorial judgment remain required. Preserve material qualifications even when a cleaner layout would result from hiding them.
